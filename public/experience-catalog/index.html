WoodlarkThe Light Room

Ten sheets from the Hybrid Series, shown whole. Each is supplied in 8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets, and each carries its code in the corner of the photograph.

The swatch you specify is the wall you receive.

  1. H-30

    • Almost no figure at all — a fine, even texture that reads as one quiet field.
    • For long runs where the joinery should recede: full-height wardrobe fronts, corridor panelling.
    • Holds an even tone as light crosses it; the texture only shows up close.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  2. H-1

    • Broad cathedral arches rise through tight straight grain, then settle again.
    • A face grain for doors and headboards, where one full arch can sit centred.
    • The arches catch light before the ground does, so the figure lifts as you walk past.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  3. H-14

    • The straightest sheet in this run — fine parallel lines, nothing to interrupt them.
    • Vertical runs where the eye should travel: tall panels, slim door leaves, reveals.
    • Reads almost as flat colour from across a room; the lines appear as you approach.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  4. H-49

    • Fine dark pinstripes run the full length against a warmer ground.
    • Suits narrow, repeated elements — shutter fronts, slatted screens, cabinet banks.
    • The stripes sharpen under directional light and soften under diffuse.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  5. H-13

    • Open, weathered texture with a grey cast — the pore is visible rather than filled.
    • Where a surface should look worked rather than polished: island fronts, feature walls.
    • The pore holds shadow, so raking light gives it depth that flat light removes.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  6. H-74

    • Very fine, unbroken vertical lines with a faint silver sheen.
    • Large uninterrupted planes where a join would show: reception fronts, lift lobbies.
    • Turns cool and metallic under directional light, warmer and quieter under diffuse.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  7. H-80

    • Dense swirling figure, close-packed eyes and rings, active across the full sheet.
    • One panel is enough — a feature door, a headboard, the back of a bar.
    • The figure moves as you move; the same sheet reads differently from two paces away.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  8. H-42

    • Fine straight grain with a subtle stripe, over a deep red-brown ground.
    • Warm rooms — libraries, dining panelling, furniture that should read as solid.
    • The red deepens under warm light and greys off under daylight; view it in both.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  9. H-59

    • Restless, wavy figure that swirls and doubles back across the width.
    • Best in short lengths where the movement can be read whole: drawer fronts, cabinet doors.
    • Light picks out the crests, so the figure appears to shift as the light moves.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets

  10. H-16

    • Cathedral figure in a cool grey, dropping to near-black below the split.
    • Dark schemes that still need grain: media walls, bar fronts, deep joinery.
    • Nearly reads as a solid dark until light rakes it and the grain comes up.

    8 × 4 ft and 10 × 4 ft sheets
